How to write an ATS-friendly resume in 2026

GTGetHiredToday Editorial · [Publish date] · 8 min read

Most employers use an applicant tracking system to collect and search applications. Your resume needs to read cleanly for that software and for the person who opens it next. The good news: the same habits help both.

1. Use a simple layout

Stick to one or two columns, real text instead of images, and clear section breaks. Tables, text boxes and icons can scramble the order your details are read in.

2. Mirror the job's keywords

If the ad says "stakeholder management", use that exact phrase where it's true for you. Recruiters search for the words in their own job description.

3. Use standard headings

"Experience", "Education" and "Skills" are understood everywhere. Creative headings like "My journey" may not be recognized.

4. Save in the right format

A text-based PDF works for most systems. When an employer asks for Word, send a .docx. Name the file with your full name and the role.
